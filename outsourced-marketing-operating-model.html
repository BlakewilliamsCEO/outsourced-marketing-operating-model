Caveat — what this model assumes

Everything below is calibrated to today's GTM and product shape: sales-led, outbound-carried, paid demand feeding a human close.

If we move toward a PLG fast start — MCP self-serve into a paywall — this changes drastically. Not at the margin. The tiers survive, because they describe how to buy. The funding order doesn't, because it describes what to buy, and in a self-serve motion the product does the demo.

The clearest tell: two lines sitting outside core today become the acquisition channel itself. If the entry point is a conversation inside Claude or ChatGPT, then being the named answer in those models is distribution, and the site stops being a lead-capture surface and becomes the handoff. Outbound priced per held meeting is the wrong instrument for a funnel whose activation event is a user calling their own agent.

It also deserves its own launch, not a line in a roadmap. Almost nobody else offers this, and category-defining differentiators decay fast once the connector directories fill up. That's a lightning strike — a single dated moment built around one demo, not a feature note. The precondition is that it works: launching before a stranger can call a number and have a real conversation spends the differentiator instead of banking it.

Treating that as a separate decision on purpose. Conflating "how we buy marketing" with "which motion we're running" is how both get decided badly. The MCP activation plan is here — its Phase 2 gate is the trigger that would make this rewrite live.

How I made the calls below

From a what-the-business-needs perspective, I split marketing activity into two groups: work that punishes slow, moderate, or unfocused execution harshly — and work that can be done slowly and reasonably well without deep expertise.

Two questions get us there.

What does 90 days of mediocre, slow execution cost us — and does it actually extend time to value capture?

This isn't about whether an activity matters. It's about what accrues while below-average or overwhelmed execution is driving the outcome. SEO takes a year regardless; a mediocre quarter there is "meh." Paid ads feed the business — one weak month and the entire company feels it.

What it isolatesThe activities where we know we want focused experts dialing us in.
Can we tell it's clearly broken as non-experts — and do we have the capacity to fix it?

Both halves matter. Nearly anyone can tell when a landing page is garbage, build three versions, test them in parallel, and optimize. That's detectable and fixable in-house.

Events are the counterexample. We can tell exactly what bad looks like and we're creative enough to put on great ones — but the logistics of executing them well are a monster that swallows the bandwidth of a small team.

What it isolatesDetectable, not fixable at our current capacity. That's an outsource for a completely different reason than paid media is.

Three tiers

Tier1

Buy the best, buy it fast

Daily burn, palpable failure. The gap between a strong vendor and an adequate in-house team is the entire budget.

Performance-based SLA · monthly review · short kill clause · no commitment past 120 days
Tier2

Buy capacity, not genius

The constraint is throughput, not brilliance. Quality floor matters; ceiling doesn't.

Priced per unit · measured on volume shipped · easily swapped · no commitment past 180 days
Tier3

Call in expertise for a defined purpose

Episodic work with long payback or a defined endpoint. We want genuine experts, on an engagement rather than a retainer — they raise the ceiling, we share the execution burden, the capability stays with us.

Project fees, not retainers

One function, one vendor, one number

Each vendor owns a single leg of the engagement. Not because coordination is easier that way — it isn't — but because it's the only structure where failure is attributable. A vendor holding three functions can always explain a bad number with another function's performance, and we have no way to test the claim. A vendor holding one function has one number and no alibi.

The second-order effect matters more

Agencies staff to risk. A sprawling multi-function retainer gets a senior lead over junior execution, because revenue is diversified across lines and no single miss threatens the account.

A single-leg engagement on a 120-day clock has every dollar riding on one visible number. That's the account they put their best people on.

Pair that with the time bound and renewal depends entirely on a number we can see, rather than on the relationship.

This is also the direct answer to the finger-pointing concern: finger-pointing requires shared ownership of an outcome. When each vendor owns one leg and one number, there's nothing to point at. Coordination overhead is real and we absorb it internally — a staffing question with a known answer, not a structural risk.

We don't have to do all of it

The tiers describe how to buy each function. They don't say which ones to buy. Those are separate decisions, and conflating them is how an outsourcing budget arrives at a number nobody can defend.

Most of the impact sits under a small number of surfaces. Past those, cost per unit stays flat while return falls off — diminishing returns at non-diminishing spend.

The failure mode

Not picking a bad vendor. Funding eight competent lines instead of three excellent ones, and watching no single number move.

The test for adding a line

Does it move one of the core four's numbers, or does it produce its own number we've explicitly decided matters? If neither, it's a line item surviving on plausibility.

Tier 3 — expertise for a defined purpose

Defined scope · defined finish · project fee

AI search visibility (GEO)

Tool + operator, not an agency

LLMs mostly cite third-party sources rather than vendor sites — review platforms, community, industry publications — and the mix differs by model. The fast path into an answer is being named inside sources the models already trust, not becoming a trusted source ourselves.

The engagement: a citation-tracking platform (Profound or an equivalent at better commercial terms) as owned infrastructure to build the target list from our money prompts, plus a placement consultant on a defined engagement, measured in inclusions landed in target-list sources. This is mention placement, not link building — a link from a cited page does nothing if we aren't named in the passage being read.

Measured byCitation share on our money prompts, tracked weekly. Rare for this function to produce a number a non-expert can read, which matters given everything above turns on detectability.

High-volume original thought leadership

No vendor shape

The shops producing content at a ceiling we can't match internally price above what makes sense at our stage. The shops whose pipeline is the moat won't sell us the pipeline. There's no available vendor shape here — which is why the GEO line is scoped as placement into already-cited sources rather than production.

Revisit when either the budget or the internal capacity changes.

The through-line

Every line is bought against a number we can read, on a clock short enough to act on.

Tiers decide how we buy. The funding order decides what we buy. One function per vendor makes the result attributable — and the scoreboard stays ours, so the whole thing stays enforceable.
